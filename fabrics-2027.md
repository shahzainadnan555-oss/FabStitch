# Fabrics for 2027 — A Sourcing and Design Reference

*Compiled 11 September 2026. Covers Spring/Summer 2027 and Autumn/Winter 2027-28, plus home and contract textiles.*

---

## How to read this document

A calendar year is not how the textile trade thinks. 2027 splits into two buying seasons, and they pull in opposite directions:

| Season | Shown at trade fairs | In stores | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SS 27** (Spring/Summer 2027) | Feb 2026 (Première Vision, Milano Unica, Texworld) | Jan–Jun 2027 | Ultralight, breathable, linen and cotton blends, sheer, fluid |
| **AW 27/28** (Autumn/Winter 2027-28) | Sept 2026 — Première Vision Paris ran 1–3 Sept at Paris Nord Villepinte | Jul–Dec 2027 | Heavier, textured, brushed and felted, colour-led |

Home textiles run on their own clock: **Heimtextil 2027** opens in Frankfurt on 12 January 2027, and its Trends 27/28 forecast was released on 7 September 2026.

If you are sourcing now, SS 27 fabric is already committed at most mills and AW 27/28 is where your open capacity sits.

---

## The two forces shaping the year

**One.** Buyers have stopped asking for new fibres and started asking for better versions of old ones. Première Vision's read on AW 27/28 is that mills are putting technology inside classic constructions and reworking traditional skills, rather than launching novelty materials. The season theme is *Living Matter*, split into three scenarios — *Matter of Time* (longevity, classical references, materials that age well), *Wild Matter* (organic variation, irregular texture) and a technology-facing third strand.

**Two.** Surface beats sheen. AW 27/28 favours brushed, felted and visibly fibrous finishes over smooth high-shine. Expect more weight, more texture, and less tolerance for perfectly uniform cloth.

Underneath both: the EU Digital Product Passport, PFAS restrictions on water repellents, and microfibre-shedding scrutiny are now commercial facts, not compliance theory. Any fabric you commit to for 2027 needs its paperwork to travel with it.

# PART 1 — Spring/Summer 2027 fabrics

## 1.1 Linen and linen blends

The backbone of the season. Trade shows pointed to ultralight, breathable cloth built on linen and cotton, in a bright light palette.

| Fabric | Composition | Weight | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| European flax linen | 100% flax | 120–200 gsm | Shirting, trousers, dresses, jackets |
| Linen–cotton | 55/45 or 70/30 | 140–220 gsm | Casual shirting, chore jackets, bottoms |
| Linen–silk | 70% linen / 30% mulberry silk | 130–180 gsm | Knitwear, fluid dresses, resort |
| Linen–viscose / linen–lyocell | 55/45 | 130–170 gsm | Drapey trousers, soft tailoring |

**Why it holds.** Flax needs little irrigation, grows in Western Europe, and carries the European Flax certification that buyers now ask for by name. Servizi e Seta's *Linsee* — 70% linen, 30% mulberry silk — shows where the premium end is going: wrinkle-free, rainproof, stretchy and light, with certified flax.

**Watch for.** Linen supply is tight in strong flax years and prices move fast. Lock volume early. Blends with 20–30% cotton or viscose cut the creasing complaints that drive returns.

## 1.2 Cotton — light, open, textured

| Fabric | Notes |
|---|---|
| Poplin and voile | Fine-count, high-twist, for crisp translucent shirting |
| Organdy / organza cotton | Stiff sheer, used in layering and volume |
| Cotton bouclé yarn | Monticolor's *Riccio* is a 100% cotton bouclé for knitwear — volume without weight |
| Seersucker and crinkle | Puckered surface, no pressing, strong for hot-climate markets |
| Slub and khadi-effect | Irregular yarn reads as handmade, fits the craft direction |

Cotton in SS 27 is about openness — you should be able to see light through it — and about yarn irregularity rather than smooth uniform surface.

## 1.3 Silk and the ethereal group

Classic luxury textiles are back in volume. Chiffon, taffeta and silk are in a resurgence, and womenswear is running on sheer chiffon, delicate lace and organza.

| Fabric | What it is | Where it lands |
|---|---|---|
| **Chiffon** | Plain-weave, high-twist yarns, sheer and floating. 8–12 momme | Layered dresses, blouses, scarves |
| **Georgette** | Like chiffon but crinkled and slightly heavier. Better drape | Dresses, wide trousers |
| **Organza** | Sheer but stiff, holds shape | Volume sleeves, overlays, structured ruffles |
| **Taffeta** | Crisp plain weave with a papery rustle | Occasion wear, full skirts, lightweight outerwear |
| **Crepe de chine** | Matte, fluid, hangs close to the body | Slip dresses, shirting, linings |
| **Habotai** | Thin, smooth, cheap silk. 8–12 momme | Linings, scarves |
| **Dupion / shantung** | Slubby, irregular, high sheen | Jackets, occasion, home decor |
| **Silk bouclé** | Loro Piana's *Silkbreeze* — 99% silk, 1% bouclé yarn, lightly elasticized, structured enough to give a garment volume, with natural silk lustre | Elevated knitwear, jackets |

**Practical note.** Sheer sells the look and kills the margin — every chiffon dress needs a lining, a facing or a slip, and that doubles your fabric consumption. Price it in at the costing stage.

## 1.4 Summer wools and tailoring

Tailoring is not going away, it is getting lighter and stretchier.

- **Merino + Roica stretch** — Reda's *Milleottocentosessantacinque* pairs merino wool with Roica elastomer for men's tailoring, and delivers wrinkle resistance, elasticity, comfort and durability.
- **Wool–silk bi-stretch** — Vitale Barberis Canonico's *Cosmic* blends wool and silk, using natural bi-stretch to get light weight, crease resistance and comfort, with high breathability for summer clothing.
- **Wool–linen and wool–mohair open weaves** — 180–240 gsm, for unstructured jackets.
- **Super 110s–130s tropical wool** — the safe commercial core for summer suiting.

The direction: 200–260 gsm suiting with 1–3% elastane or Roica, sold on comfort and recovery rather than on Super number.

## 1.5 Knitwear — the year-round shift

Knitwear is moving away from heavy winter structures toward breezy year-round textures, with open stitches, lightweight gauges and sheer knits gaining serious market share in both menswear and womenswear.

| Type | Detail |
|---|---|
| Open-stitch / pointelle / crochet-effect | 7–12 gauge, cotton, linen or blends. Reads resort |
| Fine-gauge semi-sheer | 14–18 gauge, silk, silk-cotton, fine merino. Layering pieces |
| Cotton and linen bouclé | Volume without weight |
| Lightweight merino (17.5–19.5 micron) | Menswear layering, works through the shoulder seasons |

Menswear is the growth story here — featherweight gauges and strategic layering pieces are replacing the heavy jumper.

## 1.6 Performance, swim and next-to-skin

Hyosung's SS 27 forecast built three mega trends — **Blue Wellness** (water-based recovery rituals, outdoor swimming, cold plunge), **Climate Resilience** (temperature-regulating tech, textile-to-textile recycling, travel to cooler places), and **Modern Retro** (90s sportswear and preppy colour blocking).

The fabric themes that follow from it:

- **Stretch-woven compression** for next-to-skin baselayers — "barely-there performance"
- **Swimwear fabric that crosses over** into city wear and home lounging
- **Cooling constructions** aimed at long-lasting fresh feel, sold year-round rather than as a summer feature
- **Ultra-light semi-sheer knit** for morning-to-evening lounge
- **Retro sportif** — piqué, mesh, tricot, colour-blocked ribs pulled from soccer and basketball kit

Fibre side: recycled and bio-based spandex, nylon and polyester. Hyosung has moved its regen BIO elastane, made from sugarcane, into production, and announced a fully integrated bio-spandex plant in Vietnam in September 2026. Bio-based stretch stops being a capsule story in 2027 and becomes orderable volume.

# PART 2 — Autumn/Winter 2027-28 fabrics

## 2.1 The brushed and felted group

The season rejects smooth. Fabrics are brushed, felted, or left with enough surface character that you can still see the fibre.

| Fabric | Composition | Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Melton** | Wool or wool blend, heavily felted and cropped | 500–800 gsm | Coats. Cut edges need no finishing |
| **Boiled wool** | Knitted then shrunk | 350–550 gsm | Jackets, unlined coats, soft tailoring |
| **Double-face wool** | Two faces bonded or woven, reversible | 400–700 gsm | Unlined coats, the premium outerwear staple |
| **Brushed flannel** | Wool or cotton, raised nap | 250–400 gsm | Shirting, trousers, suiting |
| **Mohair and alpaca blends** | Long hair fibre, halo effect | 300–600 gsm | Coats, statement knits |
| **Casentino** | Wool with a curled ricciolo surface | 600+ gsm | Heritage coating, reads craft |

## 2.2 Suede-touch and dry-hand surfaces

Première Vision flagged moleskins, suedes and peach-skins as a key strength — sometimes blended with wool or cellulosic fibres, supple and firm at the same time.

- **Moleskin** — dense sueded cotton twill, 280–360 gsm, workwear trousers and jackets
- **Peach-skin** — microfibre polyester or poly-cotton sanded to a dry powdery hand
- **Suede-effect wovens** — poly-nylon microfibre, brushed both sides
- **Real suede and leather** — plus opulent calf-hair finishes at the premium end of *The New Utility* direction

## 2.3 Corduroy, velvet and pile

| Fabric | Detail |
|---|---|
| Fine-wale corduroy | 16–21 wale, 280–340 gsm. Shirting and trousers |
| Jumbo cord | 4–8 wale, 350–450 gsm. Outerwear and statement trousers |
| Cotton velveteen | Short dense pile, matte |
| Silk-viscose velvet | Fluid, lustrous. Occasion and interiors |
| Crushed and panné velvet | Directional pile, irregular light |

## 2.4 Structured classics reinterpreted

- **Herringbone and Donegal tweed** — bigger repeats, brighter neps
- **Tartan and check** — reworked in oversized scale and off-register colour
- **Gabardine and covert** — technical wool with membrane backing for city outerwear
- **Jacquard and damask deconstruction** — damask motifs broken, blurred or partially rendered, one of the motif families gaining traction for AW 27

## 2.5 Technical outerwear

- **Recycled nylon ripstop**, 40–90 denier, with PFAS-free DWR
- **Membrane laminates** — 2L, 2.5L and 3L, with the membrane specified for PFAS compliance
- **Wool-technical hybrids** — wool face, membrane middle, mesh back
- **Down-alternative fills** and recycled polyester wadding

High-performance technical nylon sits alongside premium leather, suede and calf-hair as the structural counterweight to all the sheer, lightweight cloth. Both halves of the wardrobe are running at once.

## 2.6 Denim in 2027

- Lighter weights continue: 8–11 oz for the volume market, 12–14 oz for premium
- Recycled cotton content at 20–40%, blended with virgin for strength
- Laser and ozone finishing replacing stonewash and hand-sanding
- Hemp-cotton denim at 15–30% hemp, for hand feel and water-use story
- Selvedge and rigid for the heritage end, tied to the *Matter of Time* longevity theme

# PART 3 — Fibres and yarns to know

## 3.1 Recycled

| Fibre | What it is | Where it stands for 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| **rPET (bottle-based)** | Mechanically recycled PET | Commodity. Feedstock competition with food packaging is pushing prices up |
| **Textile-to-textile polyester** | Chemically depolymerised post-consumer textiles | Scaling. Circ separates polyester and cotton cellulose from blended waste via hydrothermal process |
| **Recycled nylon** | Post-industrial and post-consumer PA6 | Established in swim and outerwear |
| **Circulose** | Cellulose chemically recovered from pre- and post-consumer textiles, pulped as MMCF feedstock | Back in production after the Renewcell bankruptcy |
| **Recycled cotton** | Mechanically shredded | Limited to ~30% blend by fibre length loss |

## 3.2 Man-made cellulosics

- **Lyocell (TENCEL)** — closed-loop solvent spinning, 95%+ solvent recovery. Soft, strong wet, low shrink. The default sustainable cellulosic
- **Modal** — softer than viscose, better wet strength, for jersey and intimates
- **Cupro** — silk-like handle from cotton linter, used for linings
- **Viscose** — still the volume fibre, but chain-of-custody on wood pulp is now a buyer requirement

## 3.3 Bio-based and next-gen

| Fibre | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Bio-based elastane** | Sugarcane (Hyosung regen BIO) | In production, Vietnam capacity coming online |
| **PBS-based polyester** | Kintra — biobased biodegradable polybutylene succinate from renewable feedstock, spun into yarn for knits and wovens | Pilot to early commercial |
| **BioFleax** | Leaf Bio — agricultural waste and recycled cotton converted to FDCA, combined with bio-glycol into plant-based polyester. Soft, breathable, 3–5x better moisture management and 60% faster drying than PET, naturally antibacterial and UV resistant | Early commercial |
| **Kelsun** | Keel Labs seaweed fibre, used by Outerknown and Stella McCartney | Capsule volumes |
| **SeaCell** | Biodegradable fibre, roughly 19% seaweed | Niche, intimates and loungewear |
| **Spider-silk analogue** | Spintex spins silk from a water-based solution of dissolved silk fibres, including post-consumer waste streams, at lower energy than plastic fibre formation | R&D to pilot |
| **Agri-waste fibres** | AltMat's Altag, made from agricultural residue via mechanical, chemical and enzymatic processing | Pilot |
| **Hemp** | SEFF uses Nano-Pulse water-based energy pulses to separate hemp fibre from stalk; Felde removes lignin for softer hand | Scaling into mainstream spinning |

**Realistic read.** For 2027 bulk orders, you can buy rPET, recycled nylon, lyocell, hemp and bio-based elastane at volume. Seaweed, spider silk and agri-waste fibres are still capsule and marketing material, not your main programme.

# PART 4 — Home and contract textiles

Heimtextil's Trends 27/28, developed with Milan design platform Alcova, is titled **Home-Maxxing — The Performance of Everyday Living**. The tension it identifies: as sleep, wellness and routines get optimised, people want homes that offer rest rather than one more thing to perform at. Textiles sit in the middle of it, regulating light, absorbing sound and filtering air while still needing to feel good and last.

Three directions:

**Staged Competence** — technical expertise put on display. Technical codes, symbols and micrographics used as decoration. Cool blues, mineral shades, acid yellow. Think ripstop, mesh, printed spec-sheet motifs, performance upholstery worn as a badge.

**Inside, Out** — construction made visible. Fastenings, seams, zips and screws treated as design features instead of being hidden. Deep charcoals, saturated red, cool green-grey. Exposed selvedges, contrast overlocking, modular covers.

**Hyper Presence** — the counterweight. Texture, scent and the way materials age, prioritised over anything measurable. Natural fibres, beeswax-treated fabrics, richly tactile surfaces. Deep rust, ochre, dusty pink.

**Fabrics that follow:**

| Application | 2027 direction |
|---|---|
| Upholstery | Bouclé, heavy linen, wool felt, performance weaves with 40,000+ Martindale |
| Curtain and sheer | Open-weave linen, technical voile, acoustic-rated drapes |
| Bedding | Long-staple cotton percale and sateen, washed linen, lyocell blends |
| Rugs | Undyed wool, jute, recycled PET pile |
| Contract | Inherently FR polyester, bleach-cleanable vinyl alternatives, acoustic felt from rPET |

Heimtextil's prior season theme, *Craft is a verb*, put knots, irregular dyeing, visible seams and asymmetrical finishes at the centre as a deliberate counterpoint to AI-assisted design perfection. That handmade signal carries straight into 27/28 through the Hyper Presence direction.

# PART 5 — Colour and finish

## AW 27/28 colour

Première Vision worked with predictive analytics firm Heuritech on the AW 27/28 palette. Colour is being used to reveal grain, texture and surface irregularity rather than to sit flat on top of cloth. The palette runs to nuanced neutrals with cool digital undertones.

The *Wild Matter* range: earthy greens, pigmented greys, and shades drawn from living systems. A light sage green replaces the tinted greys of recent seasons. Dark greens derived from dye plants. An algae green, warmer and slightly yellowed, applied to velvety and textured surfaces across both synthetics and naturals. **Algae Gel** is the signal colour, carried from fluid garments through to footwear.

Across seasons, cool digital greens have thinned out since AW 25-26 in favour of warmer vegetal greens.

For SS 27, the palette is brighter and lighter, with warm sandy, clay, ochre and terracotta earth tones giving depth across a range of fabrics.

## Print and finish

- Saturated ornamented work is replacing the muted palettes of the post-2023 cycle — paisley revival, damask deconstruction, painterly floral, Japanese folk reinterpretation
- Sublimation is expanding past polyester sportswear into elevated swimwear, athleisure and contemporary womenswear, as polyester blends and treated cellulosics widen the substrate range
- Digital print growth is concentrating at two ends: short-run direct-to-fabric for independents, and high-throughput pigment digital for mass production. Mid-volume digital is getting squeezed by rotary screen pricing
- Specify to Coloro or Pantone TPX from the start on painterly work. Colour accuracy carries the whole direction and mills will not rescue a loose spec

# PART 6 — Sourcing checklist

Before you commit to any 2027 fabric:

1. **Certification** — European Flax for linen, GRS or RCS for recycled content, OEKO-TEX or bluesign for chemistry, FSC or PEFC chain of custody for cellulosics
2. **PFAS** — get written confirmation on DWR and membrane chemistry. Restrictions are already live in several markets
3. **Digital Product Passport readiness** — your mill needs to supply substrate, dye, process and traceability data in a form you can pass downstream
4. **Minimums and lead time** — novelty constructions carry high MOQs and long lead times. Sample early, order later
5. **Shrinkage and colourfastness** — brushed, felted and textured AW fabrics move in wash and press. Test before you cut
6. **Lining consumption** — sheer SS fabrics roughly double your metreage. Cost it properly
